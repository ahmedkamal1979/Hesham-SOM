## 1. Introduction to dermatopathology

**Three layers.** Epidermis, dermis, subcutaneous fat.

| Layer | Thickness |
|---|---|
| Epidermis | Under 0.1 mm on the eyelids, about 1 mm on acral sites |
| Dermis | 1 mm on the face, about 4 mm on the back |
| Subcutaneous fat | Up to several cm. Thick in the abdominal wall, thin in the eyelids. Females: pubic and thigh skin. Males: upper arm and posterior neck |

**Epidermis.** Keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium. Main cell: keratinocyte. Dendritic cells: melanocytes, Langerhans cells, Merkel cells.

**The four keratinocyte layers, bottom to top.**

| Layer | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Basal | Single layer, palisaded nuclei. Site of mitosis and regeneration (60% of basal cells proliferate). Fixed to the basal lamina by hemidesmosomes |
| Spinous (prickle) | Postmitotic. Gives the epidermis its structure. Keratin filament and desmosome complex, cornified envelope starts |
| Granular | Keratin formation and water barrier. Keratohyaline granules contain profilaggrin, which becomes filaggrin (keratin matrix protein). Filaggrin breaks down into sun-shielding and water-binding products |
| Horny | No nuclei. Protective. Basket weave look (orthokeratosis) because its contents are not fixed by formalin and wash away in processing |

**Odland bodies.** Released from the upper spinous layer, empty into the intercellular space at the granular layer. Lipid rich and hydrophobic, so they repel water.

**The three dendritic cells.**

| Cell | Origin and site | Job | Marker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Melanocyte | Neural crest. Basal layer, clear cytoplasm | Makes melanin, protects from UV | S-100, SOX10, HMB-45, Melan A |
| Langerhans cell | Bone marrow. Mid and upper epidermis, clear cytoplasm | Antigen presenting cell. More of them in contact dermatitis | CD1a |
| Merkel cell | Basement membrane level, desmosomal contact with keratinocytes | Light touch | CK20 (not seen on H&E) |

Lecturer's question: melanocytes and Langerhans cells both have clear cytoplasm. What separates them on H&E is location.

**Melanin.** Made from tyrosine, then DOPA, then dopaquinone, then eumelanin. The melanocyte passes melanosomes to keratinocytes, which store them over the nucleus so UV is absorbed before it reaches DNA. Melanin turns radiative energy into heat. Other causes of skin color: carotenoids (epidermis), oxyhaemoglobin (dermal capillaries), deoxyhaemoglobin (dermal venules).

**Desmosomes.** Attachment plaque, keratin (tono) filaments, desmoglein and desmocollin. They break and reform all the time so keratinocytes can move upward in a controlled way.

**Dermis (1 to 4 mm).** Papillary dermis: thin loose type III collagen, less eosinophilic, holds the superficial vascular plexus. Reticular dermis: dense irregular type I collagen, eosinophilic, holds the deep plexus and more elastic fibres.

**Subcutis.** Mature fat, fibrous septa, small and medium vessels, nerves. Insulation and energy store.

**Appendages.** Hair: temperature control and sweat evaporation. Sebaceous glands: sebum to lubricate skin and hair. Eccrine glands: temperature control. Apocrine glands: scent glands, fully developed at puberty.

**Mucosa versus skin.** In mucosa parakeratosis is normal, there is no basket weave and no granular layer (unless traumatised), keratinocytes are pale from glycogen, no pilosebaceous units, plasma cells are normal in the submucosa, salivary glands may be seen.

**Microscopic terms.**

| Term | Meaning | Example shown |
|---|---|---|
| Acanthosis | Thick spinous layer | Warty lesion |
| Hyperkeratosis | Thick horny layer | Warty lesion |
| Orthokeratosis | Normal basket weave horny layer | Pemphigus vulgaris |
| Parakeratosis | Nuclei kept in the horny layer | Psoriasis |
| Hypergranulosis | Thick granular layer | Lichen planus |
| Epidermal atrophy | Thin epidermis, no rete ridges | Lichen sclerosus et atrophicus |
| Acantholysis | Keratinocytes separate, desmosomes disrupted | Pemphigus vulgaris (suprabasal cleft, hemidesmosomes intact) |
| Spongiosis | Fluid between cells | Spongiotic dermatitis |
| Exocytosis | Inflammatory cells in a spongiotic epidermis | Spongiotic dermatitis |
| Reticular degeneration | Fluid between and inside cells, cells rupture, meshwork remains | Spongiotic dermatitis |
| Ballooning degeneration | Fluid inside cells after injury | Basal layer in lichen planus |

**Blisters.** Fluid filled cavity, intraepidermal or subepidermal. Vesicle under 0.5 cm, bulla over 0.5 cm. Five mechanisms: spongiosis with exocytosis, reticular degeneration, acantholysis, cytolysis (friction or heat), basement membrane zone disruption (ballooning of the basal layer).

## 2. Spongiotic dermatitis (eczema)

**Definition.** Dermatitis is inflammation of the skin. Clinically erythema and itching. Histologically spongiosis plus inflammation (perivascular in the dermis and moving into the epidermis as exocytosis). Reticular degeneration shows clinically as an intraepidermal vesicle.

**Classified by stage, cause and morphology.**

| Stage | Epidermis | Dermis |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | Normal stratum corneum, normal thickness, inter and intracellular oedema, reticular degeneration, intraepidermal vesicles | Oedema, perivascular lymphocytes and histiocytes |
| Subacute | Regeneration starts: epidermal hyperplasia, smaller vesicles, areas of spongiosis and exocytosis | |
| Chronic | Thick stratum corneum, marked hyperplasia, no spongiosis or vesicles | Fibroblastic reaction (from itching and rubbing) |

**By cause.** Endogenous: atopic, seborrheic, discoid (nummular), dyshidrotic (pompholyx), autosensitization (Id) reaction. Exogenous: contact (allergic and irritant), infective, asteatotic.

**In children.** Atopic (the most common, usually from 6 months to 5 years), contact, dyshidrotic, seborrheic (cradle cap).

**Atopic dermatitis.**
- Atopy: predisposition to asthma, allergic rhinitis and atopic dermatitis. 70% have a family history.
- Chronic, relapsing, itchy.
- Triggers: house dust mites, animals, moulds, food allergy, topical irritants, secondary infection (staph, herpes, tinea).
- Pathogenesis: genetic. Loss of tight junctions through a filaggrin loss of function mutation or reduced claudin-1. Type I hypersensitivity.
- Type I steps. First exposure: antigen presenting cell, TH2 cell, B cell makes IgE which coats mast cells, eosinophils recruited. Second exposure: antigen binds IgE, mast cells degranulate, IL-5 activates eosinophils, epithelium is damaged.
- Sites: infants and toddlers on the face, elbows and knees (crawling). Older children in the flexures.
- Acute: red, dry, itchy patches, blisters, oozing, crusting. Microscopy: acute dermatitis with intraepidermal vesicles that rupture when scratched.
- Chronic: lichenification (thick skin with prominent markings, sometimes dark). Microscopy: chronic dermatitis, which is lichen simplex chronicus, with marked hyperplasia and dermal fibrosis.
- Prognosis: 50% clear by 3 years, 66% by 6 years, 90% by 20 years.

**Seborrheic dermatitis.**
- Babies under 3 months and adults 30 to 60.
- Greasy, white or yellow flaky scales on red patches. Scalp (dandruff, cradle cap), eyebrows, eyelids, forehead, nasolabial folds, chest, creases.
- Malassezia (Pityrosporum orbiculare). Stages: trigger (stress, androgens, yeast), barrier disruption with excess sebum, inflammation, visible scales.
- Adults at risk: Parkinson's disease, facial paralysis, obesity, zinc deficiency, HIV.
- Parkinson's: dopamine loss and autonomic dysfunction increase sebum, sebum feeds the yeast.
- Zinc deficiency: the barrier collapses and Malassezia penetrates deeply.
- HIV: CD4 depletion frees the microflora, and malabsorption causes zinc deficiency.
- Microscopy: subacute dermatitis, spongiosis in the epidermis and follicle epithelium, scales with neutrophils in dilated follicular ostia.

**Pompholyx (dyshidrotic eczema).** Teenagers and young adults. Itchy vesicles or bullae on hands and feet, bilateral, often in summer. Possible causes: abnormal sweating, nickel allergy, stress. Microscopy: acute spongiotic dermatitis with intraepidermal vesicles. You know it is acral skin by the thick stratum corneum.

**Contact dermatitis.**

| | Allergic | Irritant |
|---|---|---|
| Causes | Poison ivy, nickel, rubber, cosmetics, fragrances | Chemical burns (acids, alkalis), cosmetics, detergents. In infants: diaper rash from urine and stool |
| Mechanism | Type IV, cell mediated, needs earlier sensitisation (memory helper T cells, then macrophages and cytotoxic T cells) | Not immune. Removes lipids, damages membranes, denatures keratin |
| Where it starts | Papillary dermis, then lower epidermis | Upper epidermis |
| Spongiosis | Marked, with vesicles | Slight, no vesicles |
| Ballooning | Slight | Marked |
| Necrotic keratinocytes | Absent | Present |
| Cells | Lymphocytes and eosinophils | Neutrophils |
| Erosions | Usually absent | May be present |

Nickel is one of the commonest causes in children: snaps in infants; watches, chains, belt buckles (around the umbilicus) and earrings in older children. Weak irritants can look like allergic contact dermatitis.

**Nummular eczema.** Coin-shaped, scaly, crusted, itchy plaques. Subacute spongiotic dermatitis.

## 3. Ophthalmology examination

**Opening.** Hand washing, greeting, introduction, consent, positioning.

**Steps.** Inspection, history taking, examination, recording.

**Inspection.** Around the bed (walking aids, glasses). General (unilateral weakness, facial asymmetry). Eyes (lids, conjunctiva, sclera, pupil, iris). Examples: ptosis, red conjunctiva, pale conjunctiva, dilated pupil.

**History.** Chief complaint in the patient's own words (redness, burning, flashes of light). Past ophthalmic history: surgeries, medications, trauma. Then: sudden or gradual, painful, transient or persistent, one or both eyes, local or systemic. Field loss: disc is the blind spot, macula is central vision, retinal disease is peripheral loss, bitemporal or homonymous hemianopia, scotomas (central, arcuate, altitudinal). Triggers: sun, headlights, cinema.

**Distance visual acuity.** 6 m (20 ft). Each eye separately with the other covered. The lowest line read correctly is the acuity.

**Pinhole.** One eye then the other. If the patient reads further down, there is an uncorrected refractive error.

**Near visual acuity.** 40 cm (16 inches), glasses on if needed, one eye occluded, Jaeger text, smallest text read. No chart: newspaper text is N8, headlines N12.

**Color vision.** Ishihara plates, 24 to 38 plate edition, held at 75 cm. Detects red-green deficiency only. Four designs: vanishing, transformation, hidden digit, classification. Both eyes first (glasses on), then right eye alone, then left. Compare with the chart and record the plates missed.

**Fundus.** Taught later in ICC-A.

## 4. OSCE checklist: what the examiner ticks

1. Wash hands. Greet and introduce yourself. Explain and get consent.
2. Seat the patient at 6 m (20 ft), unless a modified chart is used.
3. Ask about glasses, contact lenses and previous eye surgery.
4. Look around the bed: walking aids, white stick, glasses. General inspection. Inspect the eye.
5. History: the lecture list plus "blurred, dimmed or distorted?"
6. Distance acuity, each eye alone: uncorrected (UCVA) first, then best corrected with glasses (BCVA). If no letters are seen: count fingers (CF), then hand movements (HM), then perception of light (PL).
7. Pinhole test.
8. Near vision at 40 cm. Record the smallest line read.
9. Color vision at 75 cm. Record the frames missed.
10. Fundus: later (ICC-A).

# High yield sheet

**Skin structure**
- Epidermis: keratinizing stratified squamous. Thinnest on eyelids, thickest on acral skin.
- Basal layer is mitosis. Spinous is structure. Granular is keratin and water barrier. Horny is protection.
- Keratohyaline granules are profilaggrin. Filaggrin products shield sun and bind water.
- Odland bodies: lipid, hydrophobic, from the upper spinous layer.
- Basket weave is a formalin processing effect.
- Melanocyte: neural crest, basal, S-100, SOX10, HMB-45, Melan A.
- Langerhans: bone marrow, mid epidermis, CD1a, antigen presenting.
- Merkel: basement membrane level, light touch, CK20, invisible on H&E.
- Melanin from tyrosine. Melanosomes sit over the keratinocyte nucleus.
- Desmosomes: desmoglein, desmocollin. Basal cells hold on by hemidesmosomes.
- Papillary dermis type III collagen and superficial plexus. Reticular dermis type I collagen and deep plexus.
- Apocrine is scent, from puberty. Eccrine is temperature.
- Mucosa: parakeratosis normal, no granular layer, glycogen, plasma cells normal.

**Terms and blisters**
- Parakeratosis in psoriasis. Hypergranulosis in lichen planus. Atrophy in LSA. Acantholysis in pemphigus vulgaris (suprabasal, hemidesmosomes intact).
- Spongiosis is between cells. Ballooning is inside cells. Reticular degeneration is both.
- Vesicle under 0.5 cm, bulla over 0.5 cm.
- Five blister mechanisms: spongiosis, reticular degeneration, acantholysis, cytolysis (friction, heat), basement membrane zone disruption.

**Eczema**
- Eczema: erythema and itch clinically, spongiosis and inflammation histologically.
- Acute: normal corneum, vesicles. Subacute: hyperplasia begins, small vesicles. Chronic: thick corneum, marked hyperplasia, no spongiosis, dermal fibrosis.
- Exogenous: contact, infective, asteatotic. The rest are endogenous.
- Atopic: commonest in children, 70% family history, filaggrin or claudin-1, type I, TH2, IgE, mast cells, IL-5, eosinophils.
- Infant sites: face, elbows, knees. Chronic rubbing gives lichenification, which is lichen simplex chronicus with dermal fibrosis.
- Atopic clears: 50% by 3, 66% by 6, 90% by 20 years.
- Seborrheic: under 3 months and 30 to 60 years, greasy scales, Malassezia, subacute picture with neutrophils in follicular ostia. Linked to Parkinson's, zinc deficiency, HIV, obesity, facial paralysis.
- Pompholyx: teens, hands and feet, summer, acute picture, thick corneum means acral skin.
- Allergic contact: type IV, nickel, lower epidermis, marked spongiosis, eosinophils.
- Irritant contact: not immune, diaper rash, upper epidermis, necrotic keratinocytes, ballooning, neutrophils.
- Nummular: coin-shaped plaques, subacute.

**Eye examination**
- Order: inspection, history, examination, recording.
- Distance acuity 6 m, each eye alone, lowest correct line. UCVA before BCVA. Then CF, HM, PL.
- Pinhole improves: uncorrected refractive error.
- Near acuity 40 cm, Jaeger. Newspaper N8, headlines N12.
- Ishihara: 75 cm, red-green only, four designs (vanishing, transformation, hidden digit, classification), both eyes first then each eye.
- Disc is the blind spot, macula is central vision, retinal disease is peripheral loss.
- Look for the white stick. Ask about glasses, lenses and eye surgery.

**Four places where your slides differ slightly from the standard references**
- Vesicle and bulla: the slide uses 0.5 cm as the cut off. Many textbooks use 1 cm. Answer with the slide's number in this course.
- Atopic dermatitis is given on the slide as a type I reaction. Standard texts describe a mixed picture (type I with a later type IV, T cell part). Answer type I for this lecturer.
- The slide writes the incidence of atopy in children as "1—20%". This looks like a typing slip for 10 to 20%, so I left it out of the quiz. Ask the lecturer if it matters.
- The slide says tight junctions are lost "within the cornified layer". Standard histology places tight junctions in the granular layer. The exam point is still filaggrin and claudin-1.
